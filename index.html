Research interests

I study the relationship between language processing and the memory, attention, and neural mechanisms that support cognition. My research asks how people construct, maintain, and retrieve representations during discourse comprehension, and how linguistic form interacts with context and cognitive demands.

I hold a joint appointment in Psychology and Linguistics at the University of South Carolina, where I direct The Language and Cognition aLab.

Current research in the lab spans four connected areas:

  • Discourse reference processingReferential form, discourse prominence, spatial representations, and the neural bases of reference.
  • Attention, memory & communicationResource competition during conversation and sustained attention in complex multitasking.
  • Language, anthropomorphism & AILinguistic framing, animacy perception, and attributions of agency and responsibility.
  • Pragmatics & social inferenceCommon ground, perspective-taking, and nonliteral interpretation.

Earlier work examined language and memory in aging and Alzheimer’s disease, as well as human reasoning and domain specialization. Explore earlier research ↗
